## 1. Microgravity and the ISS (Question 3)

> **Problem:** What is the acceleration due to gravity (g) at the altitude of the ISS (400 km)? Why do astronauts experience a state of "weightlessness" despite this gravity?

### The Concept
A common misconception is that gravity does not exist in space. In reality, gravity is the force keeping the ISS in orbit. The sensation of "weight" comes from the **Normal Force** (the ground pushing up against you). Astronauts feel weightless because the ISS is moving tangentially so fast that the Earth curves away beneath them at the same rate they fall. They are in a state of **continuous freefall**, meaning there is no Normal Force.

### The Mathematics
To prove gravity is still active, we use **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation** to find the local acceleration:

$$g = G\frac{M_E}{(R_E + h)^2}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
* **$g$**: Local acceleration due to gravity.
* **$G$**: Universal gravitational constant (**6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg²**).
* **$M_E$**: Mass of the Earth (**5.97 × 10²⁴ kg**).
* **$R_E$**: Radius of the Earth (**6378 km** or **6.378 × 10⁶ m**).
* **$h$**: Altitude of the object (**400 km** or **4.0 × 10⁵ m**).

### The Calculation
First, find the total distance from the center of the Earth ($r = R_E + h$):
* Total distance = **6778 km** = **6.778 × 10⁶ m**

Substitute the values:
$$g = (6.674 \times 10^{-11})\frac{5.97 \times 10^{24}}{(6.778 \times 10^6)^2}$$
$$g \approx 8.67 \text{ m/s}^2$$

### Conclusion
The acceleration due to gravity at the ISS is **8.67 m/s²**, which is roughly **88%** as strong as it is on the Earth's surface. The weightlessness is entirely due to orbital freefall, not an absence of gravity.

# 8. AC Voltage Equation

The current in an AC circuit is given by **I(t) = 2 sin(120πt)**. If the circuit consists of a single **50 Ω** resistor, what is the equation for the voltage **V(t)** across it?

---

## Given Data

* **Current Equation, I(t):** 2 sin(120πt) A
* **Peak Current (I₀):** 2 A
* **Angular Frequency (ω):** 120π rad/s
* **Resistance (R):** 50 Ω
* **Circuit Type:** Purely Resistive AC Circuit

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Analyze the Phase Relationship

In a purely resistive AC circuit, the voltage across the resistor and the current flowing through it are completely **in phase**. They peak and cross zero at the exact same moments. Because of this, the wave component **sin(120πt)** remains exactly the same for both the current and voltage equations.

---

### Step 2: Calculate the Peak Voltage (V₀)

We can find the maximum (peak) voltage drop across the resistor by applying Ohm's Law to the peak current value.

> **Formula:** V₀ = I₀ * R

**Calculation:**
* V₀ = 2 A * 50 Ω
* **V₀ = 100 V**

---

### Step 3: Formulate the Voltage Equation, V(t)

The standard mathematical format for alternating voltage follows the sine wave framework:

> **Formula:** V(t) = V₀ sin(ωt)

By plugging in our peak voltage (V₀ = 100 V) and matching the identical angular frequency (ω = 120π), we get:

* **V(t) = 100 sin(120πt)**

---

## Final Summary

| Parameter | Symbol / Formula | Value / Expression |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peak Current** | I₀ | **2 A** |
| **Peak Voltage** | V₀ = I₀ * R | **100 V** |
| **Voltage Equation** | V(t) = V₀ sin(ωt) | **V(t) = 100 sin(120πt)** |

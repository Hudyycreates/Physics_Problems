## Problem Statement

Two capacitors, $C_1 = 4\,\mu\text{F}$ and $C_2 = 6\,\mu\text{F}$, are connected in parallel to a **10 V** battery. 

1. What is the total charge stored on the capacitors?
2. What is the total energy stored?

---

## Given Data

* **Capacitance 1 ($C_1$):** $4\,\mu\text{F} = 4 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$
* **Capacitance 2 ($C_2$):** $6\,\mu\text{F} = 6 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$
* **Voltage ($V$):** 10 V
* **Circuit Type:** Parallel Connection

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Find the Total (Equivalent) Capacitance ($C_{\text{eq}}$)

When capacitors are connected in **parallel**, they simply add up to form the total capacitance because the total plate area increases.

$$\text{Formula: } C_{\text{eq}} = C_1 + C_2$$

**Calculation:**
$$C_{\text{eq}} = 4\,\mu\text{F} + 6\,\mu\text{F}$$
$$C_{\text{eq}} = 10\,\mu\text{F} = 10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate the Total Charge Stored ($Q_{\text{total}}$)

The fundamental relationship between charge ($Q$), capacitance ($C$), and voltage ($V$) is:

$$\text{Formula: } Q = C \cdot V$$

Using our equivalent capacitance ($C_{\text{eq}}$) and the source voltage ($V$):

**Calculation:**
$$Q_{\text{total}} = C_{\text{eq}} \times V$$
$$Q_{\text{total}} = (10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}) \times 10\,\text{V}$$
$$Q_{\text{total}} = 100 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{C} = 100\,\mu\text{C}$$

> **Result 1:** The total charge stored across the system is **100 µC** (or $1.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{C}$).

---

### Step 3: Calculate the Total Energy Stored ($U_{\text{total}}$)

The electric potential energy ($U$) stored in a capacitive system can be found using the equivalent capacitance and voltage:

$$\text{Formula: } U = \frac{1}{2} C_{\text{eq}} V^2$$

**Calculation:**
$$U_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{2} \times (10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}) \times (10\,\text{V})^2$$
$$U_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{2} \times (10 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F}) \times 100\,\text{V}^2$$
$$U_{\text{total}} = (5 \times 10^{-6}) \times 100$$
$$U_{\text{total}} = 500 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{J} = 5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{J}$$

> **Alternative Check:** You can also verify this using the charge formula $U = \frac{1}{2} Q V$:
> $$U_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{2} \times (100 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{C}) \times 10\,\text{V} = 500 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{J}$$

> **Result 2:** The total energy stored in the system is **500 µJ** (or $5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{J}$).

---

## Final Summary

| Metric | Formula Used | Final Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Equivalent Capacitance ($C_{\text{eq}}$)** | $C_1 + C_2$ | **10 µF** |
| **Total Charge ($Q_{\text{total}}$)** | $C_{\text{eq}} \cdot V$ | **100 µC** ($1.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{C}$) |
| **Total Energy ($U_{\text{total}}$)** | $\frac{1}{2} C_{\text{eq}} V^2$ | **500 µJ** ($5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{J}$) |

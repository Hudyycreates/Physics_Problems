# Problem 5: Percentage Calculation

**Question:** A measurement of time is recorded as $t=5.45 \pm 0.22$ seconds. What is the percentage uncertainty of this measurement?

---

### Where the Numbers Come From
Instead of memorizing a formula, look at what the numbers represent: you have an error of `0.22` sitting inside a total measurement of `5.45`. 
To find a percentage, we just need to answer two basic questions: 
1. What fraction of the total measurement is this error?
2. What does that fraction look like if we scale it to be "out of 100"?

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Find the fraction (Part out of Whole)**
We take our error margin and divide it by our total measurement to see what slice of the pie it represents.
* Error: $0.22 \text{ s}$
* Total: $5.45 \text{ s}$
* Fraction: $0.22 \div 5.45 \approx 0.04036$

*This means the error is roughly 0.04 times the size of the actual measurement.*

**Step 2: Scale it to a Percentage**
Since percentages are always "per 100", we take that decimal and multiply it by 100.
* $0.04036 \times 100 = 4.036\%$
* Rounding to two decimal places, we get **4.04%**.

**Final Answer:**
The error of 0.22 seconds represents a **4.04%** uncertainty in the total measurement.
